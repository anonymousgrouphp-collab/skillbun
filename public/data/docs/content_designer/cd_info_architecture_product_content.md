# Information Architecture for Product Content Study Guide

## Introduction
Information Architecture (IA) is the art and science of organizing, labeling, and structuring content within digital products (websites, applications, intranets) to help users find, understand, and interact with information effectively. For product content, good IA is crucial for user experience, ensuring clarity, usability, and discoverability. It's about creating logical pathways and meaningful relationships between pieces of content, mirroring users' mental models.

## Core Principles of Information Architecture

### 1. Content Organization
Content organization refers to how content is structured and grouped logically to create a coherent framework. This involves defining the overall structure and hierarchy of information.

*   **Definition**: Arranging content in a clear, logical, and intuitive manner.
*   **Common Structures**:
    *   **Hierarchical**: Tree-like structure, moving from general to specific (e.g., main categories with sub-categories).
    *   **Sequential**: Step-by-step linear progression, often used for processes or tutorials.
    *   **Matrix**: Allows users to navigate content along multiple dimensions (e.g., filtering products by size, color, and brand).
    *   **Database**: Dynamic content served based on user queries and metadata.
*   **Techniques**: Content audits to assess existing content, card sorting to understand user mental models for grouping content.
